## Synthesis

### Bottlenecks
1. **DNA synthesis cost** — High cost of gene-length DNA synthesis remains a primary barrier; even with 24-fold reductions, absolute costs are significant for large constructs.
2. **Assembly efficiency** — Golden Gate assembly efficiency drops sharply beyond ~2000 bp (88% → 58% at 5600 bp without selective purification).
3. **Host-specificity** — Genome integration tools evolved for particular organisms function poorly in distantly related species.
4. **Off-target effects** — All genome editing tools (ZFN, TALEN, CRISPR) exhibit off-target activity; TALENs show lower cytotoxicity but are harder to engineer.
5. **Genomic scars** — Repeated modifications accumulate scars, limiting iterative engineering.
6. **Plasmid instability** — Plasmid-based systems impose metabolic burden and are impractical for real-world deployment.
7. **Screening burden** — Identifying correctly modified clones remains labor-intensive; SuRe's IGA approach reduces but doesn't eliminate this.
8. **Data integration** — Multimodal data integration across instruments, software, and scales remains a bottleneck in regenerative medicine.
9. **Biosecurity screening** — Current nucleic acid synthesis screening relies on database matching, which AI-generated novel sequences could bypass.
10. **Delivery constraints** — Large multi-component systems (e.g., CASTs with 7 proteins + gRNA) present significant in vivo delivery challenges.

### SOTA Approaches
1. **Integrated design-build-test pipelines** — Combining computational design (Promoter/RBS/CDS Calculators), oligopool assembly, nanopore verification, and biosensor screening in a single workflow.
2. **Fourth-generation integration editors** — SSRs, transposases, CASTs, and bridge recombinases enabling gene-sized insertion without DSBs.
3. **Cloud-based platforms** — Benchling (biology-first, AI Scientist), Geneious Prime (comprehensive suite + plugin SDK), SnapGene (intuitive cloning).
4. **Low-cost hardware** — DNA Playground ($300, Arduino-based) for accessible genetic engineering.
5. **Scalable in vivo assembly** — SuRe system with logarithmic scaling of integration time.
6. **CRISPR-associated transposases (CASTs)** — RNA-guided DNA integration up to 10 kb at up to 100% efficiency in bacteria.
7. **Genetic compilers** — Cello 2.0 for transforming high-level descriptions into engineered circuits.
8. **Biosecurity by design** — Built-in safeguards, sequence screening, genetic biocontainment.

### Cost Tradeoffs
- **Benchling**: Enterprise SaaS, custom pricing (likely $50–200/user/month based on market rates)
- **Geneious Prime**: Perpetual license ~$1,000–3,000; subscription available
- **SnapGene**: Perpetual license ~$350–1,000; viewer mode free
- **DNA Playground**: $300 hardware + $33 consumable kits
- **CRISPR gRNA design**: $357–$617/sample (academic core rates)
- **CRISPR gRNA synthesis**: $667–$1,106/sample
- **NovaSeq sequencing**: $3,757–$32,113/flowcell depending on platform
- **CLIA genome sequencing**: $1,674–$2,511/sample

### Hardware Requirements
- **DNA Playground**: Arduino Mega-based, temperature-controlled heating/cooling, incubator, touchscreen. $300.
- **Standard lab**: Hot plate, incubator, ice bath, thermometer, timer (DIY alternative).
- **Computational**: Standard workstation sufficient for most design tools; HPC for large-scale NGS analysis.
- **Liquid handling automation**: For high-throughput pipelines (biofoundry-grade).

### Scalability Limits
- **SuRe**: Logarithmic scaling of integration time; demonstrated up to 4.2 Mbp, 12 elements.
- **CASTs**: Up to 10 kb insertion, 100% efficiency in bacteria; 0.1–1% in mammalian cells (evoCAST: 10–20%).
- **MAGE**: Dramatically reduced time for combinatorial mutation libraries.
- **Plasmid-based**: Limited by metabolic burden and instability.
- **Genome-integrated**: Lower expression (single-copy), genomic context effects.

### Biosecurity Governance
- **DARPA Safe Genes**: Spatial/temporal/reversible control, small molecule countermeasures, gene drive reversal.
- **Sequence screening**: Routine at DNA synthesis providers; database-matching based.
- **Genetic biocontainment**: Engineered auxotropy, kill switches.
- **AI safeguards**: Nature Biotechnology call for built-in biosecurity in generative AI tools.
- **CIRM INFR9**: Funding for OSS tools addressing data integration with biosecurity considerations.

### Failure Modes
1. **Off-target editing** — Unintended modifications at non-target loci.
2. **Assembly failure** — Low efficiency for large or repetitive constructs.
3. **Plasmid loss** — Instability in absence of selection pressure.
4. **Genomic scars** — Accumulation of residual sequences from repeated editing.
5. **Host incompatibility** — Tools failing in non-model organisms.
6. **Delivery failure** — Inability to deliver large multi-component systems in vivo.
7. **Screening escapees** — False positives in high-throughput screening.
8. **AI bypass** — Generative AI designing sequences that evade screening databases.
9. **Context effects** — Genomic integration position affecting expression.
10. **Cost overruns** — DNA synthesis and sequencing costs exceeding budgets.

### NP-Hard Problems
1. **Combinatorial assembly optimization** — Selecting optimal overhangs for many-fragment Golden Gate assembly is NP-hard; Genetic Systems Builder uses dynamic programming with beam search.
2. **Multiplexed genome engineering** — Designing optimal combinations of perturbations across genome-scale networks.
3. **Protein design** — Inverse folding and sequence-structure mapping.
4. **Metabolic pathway optimization** — Bi-level optimization (OptKnock) for predicting knockout combinations.
5. **Phylogenetic tree building** — Maximum likelihood and Bayesian inference are NP-hard.
6. **Sequence alignment** — Multiple sequence alignment is NP-hard in general.
