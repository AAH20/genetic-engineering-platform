# Cluster 6: Metabolic Engineering Biosecurity

## Overview

Biosecurity in metabolic engineering addresses the dual-use dilemma: the same tools that enable beneficial engineering (biofuels, pharmaceuticals, bioremediation) can be misused to create novel pathogens, toxins, or biological weapons. This cluster synthesizes research across 10 search dimensions covering governance, frameworks, tools, hardware, costs, scalability, and compliance.

## Synthesis: Key Bottlenecks

1. **Sequence-based screening vulnerability**: AI-assisted protein design can create synthetic homologs that evade BSS tools relying on sequence identity (Wittmann et al., 2025)
2. **List-based governance obsolescence**: DNA synthesis advances (1000+ bases, benchtop systems) have expanded feasibility beyond traditional state programs, rendering list-based approaches insufficient
3. **Fragmented oversight**: US biosecurity authorities spread across multiple agencies, contributing to slow policymaking (BMIA Sense of Congress)
4. **AI capability gap**: Rapid AI advancement in protein/nucleic acid design outpaces biosecurity framework adaptation
5. **Decentralized synthesis**: Benchtop DNA synthesis systems bypass centralized screening infrastructure
6. **Compliance measurement gap**: Lack of standardized assessment tools and data-sharing mechanisms for evaluating biosecurity compliance
7. **Cost-recovery tension**: Biosecurity funding relies on cost recovery (38%) vs. government appropriations (46%), creating misaligned incentives
8. **Insider threat**: Personnel suitability and access control remain challenging despite regulatory requirements
