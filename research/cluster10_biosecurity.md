**Wave 1 Research | Focus: Biosecurity**

## 1. Executive Summary

Integrated genetic engineering systems—combining AI-driven protein design, automated DNA synthesis, and closed-loop laboratory automation (Design–Build–Test–Learn cycles)—represent a paradigm shift in biological capability. These systems, termed "Intelligent Automated Biology" (IAB), accelerate therapeutic discovery and pandemic preparedness but simultaneously reshape the biosecurity landscape by lowering barriers to high-risk biological work. Current governance frameworks remain fragmented across national boundaries, and sequence-based screening tools face growing evasion risks from AI-assisted protein design. This report synthesizes findings across ten research dimensions to map the biosecurity challenge and identify critical bottlenecks.

## 2. Biosecurity Governance

### 2.1 International Frameworks

- **WHO Global Guidance Framework (2022)**: Sets out values, principles, and a comprehensive menu of tools for states to adapt. Explicitly addresses the full research life cycle—from funding decisions through laboratory operations to publication—and emphasizes integrating biosafety, biosecurity, and bioethics considerations. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1924238/full)

- **ISO 35001:2019 Biorisk Management Standard**: Provides a systematic framework for managing biological risks in laboratories. Serves as a foundational standard for institutional biorisk management systems. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1924238/full)

- **U.S. DURC-PEPP Policy (2024)**: The U.S. Government Policy for Oversight of Dual Use Research of Concern and Pathogens with Enhanced Pandemic Potential. Its planned implementation was superseded by Executive Order 14,292 while a revised or replacement federal policy remained under development. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1924238/full)

- **FAO Biosecurity Toolkit (2007)**: Defines biosecurity as "a strategic and integrated approach to analyse and manage risks in food safety, animal and plant life and health, and biosafety." Provides guidance for developing national biosecurity frameworks, capacity assessment tools, and risk analysis manuals. [FAO](https://www.fao.org/food/food-safety-quality/a-z-index/biosecurity/en)

- **Australian Biosecurity Act 2015**: Comprehensive legislation covering human biosecurity control orders, compliance and enforcement powers, and biosecurity risk management. Includes provisions for isolation, traveler movement measures, and ballast water management. [Legislation.gov.au](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2015A00061/2024-10-14/2024-10-14/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html)

### 2.2 National DURC Governance

A proposed national framework for managing Dual-Use Research of Concern (DURC) integrates biosecurity, public health, research governance, and emergency management. The framework comprises eleven integrated recommendations across prevention and response domains:
1. Establishing a national DURC governance structure
2. Strengthening institutional oversight and ethics review
3. Implementing laboratory biorisk management systems
4. Addressing cyberbiosecurity
5. Mitigating insider threats
6. Promoting responsible communication safeguards
7. Enhancing early detection and surveillance
8. Developing DURC-specific emergency response plans
9. Protecting first responders and healthcare workers
10. Institutionalizing risk communication and public engagement
11. Embedding continuous learning and international collaboration

[Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1924238/full)

### 2.3 Cyberbiosecurity

As high-containment laboratories rely increasingly on networked instruments, automated systems, and digital sequence data, the attack surface for cyber-enabled biological harm expands. Cyber-attacks can manipulate experimental conditions, corrupt safety systems, or exfiltrate sensitive DURC data. The convergence of cybersecurity and biosecurity—termed "cyberbiosecurity"—represents a relatively new and rapidly evolving governance concern not yet consistently integrated into DURC oversight frameworks. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1924238/full)

## 3. Dual-Use Research of Concern (DURC)

### 3.1 Expanding Threat Landscape

Advances in synthetic biology, gene editing, and artificial intelligence have expanded both the pace and diffusion of high-risk biological work, while governance systems remain fragmented across national boundaries. Benchtop DNA synthesizers that once cost hundreds of thousands of dollars are becoming accessible to smaller laboratories and even non-institutional actors, fundamentally reshaping the threat landscape. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1924238/full)

### 3.2 DNA Synthesis Evolution

Since the 1980s, DNA synthesis technology has evolved from chemical to enzymatic methods, from single-column synthesis to massively parallel chip-based approaches, and from a handful of commercial providers to a vast global market supplemented by decentralized benchtop systems. Costs have dramatically reduced while synthesizable oligonucleotide lengths have increased from a few hundred bases in 2015 to well over a thousand in 2026. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1819026/pdf)

### 3.3 Convergence of Technologies

A growing consensus in the biosecurity community is that advances in DNA synthesis, laboratory automation, computational design, and the convergence of biology with chemistry and nanotechnology have fundamentally altered the assumptions behind list-based approaches. Capabilities such as gene sequencing, gene editing, and de novo DNA synthesis, once confined to elite facilities, are increasingly embedded in commercial platforms and academic laboratories, sometimes with the help of artificial intelligence. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1819026/pdf)

### 3.4 CRISPR and Gene Editing

Gene editing tools such as CRISPR-based systems, synthetic genomics, and computationally enabled protein design have drastically changed the way scientists explore and alter biological functions. Design–build-test cycles that integrate software, automated synthesis, and high-throughput screening have accelerated experimentation and reduced reliance on slow, highly contingent trial-and-error approaches. Dual-use potential is no longer confined to rare pathogens or specialized biodefense programs: it increasingly arises within mainstream life science research and industrial biotechnology. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1819026/pdf)

## 4. Ethics and Responsible Research

### 4.1 Integrated Ethics Frameworks

The Institute of Business Ethics (IBE) Integrity and Business Ethics Framework emphasizes that integration and systems thinking ensures holistic alignment across the organisation by integrating all elements of the Integrity and Business Ethics Framework and considering the interconnected impact of decisions on people, culture, performance, stakeholders and society. It requires a systems thinking approach: understanding how decisions in one area of the organisation can have unintended consequences elsewhere. [IBE](https://www.ibe.org.uk/business-ethics-framework/integration-and-systems-thinking)

### 4.2 IEEE Ethics of Autonomous and Intelligent Systems

The IEEE Global Initiative on Ethics of Autonomous and Intelligent Systems establishes that as the use and impact of autonomous and intelligent systems (A/IS) become pervasive, societal and policy guidelines are needed for these systems to remain human-centric, serving humanity's values and ethical principles. Systems should generate audit trails recording the facts and law supporting decisions and should be amenable to third-party verification. [IEEE](https://standards.ieee.org/wp-content/uploads/import/documents/other/ead_executive_summary_v2.pdf)

### 4.3 Healthcare Ethics Integration

The IntegratedEthics™ model replaces the traditional ethics committee structure with three discrete functions: ethics consultation, preventive ethics, and ethical leadership. This model has been implemented throughout the VA health care system and provides a framework for identifying ethics quality gaps at the systems level. [Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/21507716.2012.752416)

## 5. Biosecurity Screening Tools and OSS

### 5.1 Sequence-Based Screening Vulnerabilities

Biosecurity screening software (BSS) used by most nucleic acid synthesis providers relies on sequence identity to known sequences of concern (SOCs) to flag potential hazards. A critical vulnerability has been demonstrated: AI-assisted protein design (AIPD) tools can reformulate wild-type proteins of concern (POCs) to produce synthetic homologs with minimal sequence identity to those wild types but that could nevertheless fold into structurally similar proteins, potentially retaining their function. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1858951/full)

### 5.2 Fragment Detection Limits

Testing of four BSS tools that were recently patched to enhance their AI resiliency found that two of the four tools were capable of robustly detecting fragments as short as 50 nucleotides, demonstrating screening capabilities that exceed those requested in the U.S. Framework for Nucleic Acid Synthesis. Upgraded versions of the other two tools improved performance. However, the effectiveness of sequence-based BSS is expected to decline with continuing improvement of AIPD capabilities. [bioRxiv, 2026](https://biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.04.709671v1.full-text)

### 5.3 Protein Watermarking

Google DeepMind has developed a protein watermarking system based on SynthID technology. The system creates a watermark on protein sequences themselves without compromising the protein's function. This allows new proteins designed by trusted researchers to be identified, opening everything else up to closer scrutiny. The watermark influences the probability of certain choices the AI makes, and that bias ends up systematically distributed throughout the product. [Ars Technica, 2026](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/09/google-figures-out-how-to-watermark-ai-designed-proteins)

### 5.4 Need for Alternate BSS Approaches

There is an urgent need to develop alternate BSS tools where SOCs are more directly identified (e.g., with methods that could predict a sequence's function) rather than via a correlate like sequence identity. [bioRxiv, 2026](https://biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.04.709671v1.full-text)

## 6. Hardware Requirements

### 6.1 Biological Safety Cabinets (BSCs)

BSCs are primary containment devices providing personnel, environmental, and product protection during manipulation of infectious microorganisms. Class II BSCs may be used with organisms requiring BSL-4 containment if used in a BSL-4 suit laboratory by a worker wearing a positive pressure protective suit. Key requirements include:
- HEPA filtration with bag-in/bag-out systems for hazardous materials
- Formaldehyde gas decontamination capability with airtight dampers
- Facility engineering requirements including adequate clearance (12-14 inches above cabinet)
- Building exhaust system compatibility
- Electrical consumption limits (e.g., ≤180 watts for new units)

[Thermo Fisher Scientific](http://apps.thermoscientific.com/media/LPG/BSCNewconstruction/specs-eu/Thermo%20Scientific%20MSC-Advantage%20Bid%20Specs030912.pdf) | [APSU Primary Containment Guide](https://www.apsu.edu/health-safety/Primary_containment_for_biohazards.pdf)

### 6.2 Biosecurity Access Control Hardware

Physical biosecurity systems integrate biometric access control (fingerprint, facial recognition, palm scanning), ID document scanning, CCTV/NVR systems, and visitor management. Typical hardware includes:
- Fingerprint readers (ZKTeco CR10E&M, ZK4500, ZK8500R)
- Document scanners (ZKTeco RS100, Desko PENTA)
- Network cameras and NVR systems
- Card printers and ID card readers
- Server infrastructure (Windows Server 2012/2016/2019, Intel Core i7 or above)

[ZKTeco ZKBioSecurity](https://zkteco.technology/calculator/ZKBioSecurity%C2%A0Hardware%C2%A0Suggestion%C2%A0List_V5000%204.1.0_R-20210929.pdf)

### 6.3 Laboratory Automation Hardware

Automated biofoundries require robotic systems for DNA synthesis, variant production, and high-throughput assay measurement. These platforms are capable of testing thousands of variants per day, enabling fully automated DBTL cycles. [Frontiers in Microbiology, 2025](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2025.1734561/full)

## 8. Scalability

### 8.1 Progressive Management Pathway

The FAO Progressive Management Pathway for Terrestrial Animal Biosecurity (PMP-TAB) is a stepwise approach for strengthening biosecurity management along value chains to enhance the health, resilience, and sustainability of animal sectors. A scoping review found considerable disparities in the geographic distribution of evidence related to biosecurity initiatives and differences in definitions for biosecurity, calling upon international organisations such as FAO to spearhead efforts towards a global framework. [PMC, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10451226)

### 8.2 Key Scalability Challenges

- **No one-size-fits-all approach**: Generalised standards or "one-size-fits-all" solutions are key weaknesses of current biosecurity. Implementation should be specific to the local context and its respective environment and systems.
- **Single-disease focus**: Most biosecurity initiatives are specific to a single disease and related to traditional livestock species, failing to recognise that a wide range of hazards are important.
- **Stakeholder inclusion**: All relevant stakeholders along the value chains—including public and private stakeholders, as well as formal and informal actors—must be included in the design of biosecurity initiatives.
- **Sustainability factors**: Economic, social, and environmental sustainability must be considered for long-term scalability.

[PMC, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10451226)

### 8.3 Autonomous Lab Scaling

Ginkgo Bioworks' Nebula autonomous lab is described as "the world's largest autonomous lab with the ability to run real customer science around the clock." The company is targeting to double its size in 2026, signaling rapid scaling of automated biological research infrastructure. [SEC Filing, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1830214/000162828026032093/ex991earningspr.htm)

## 9. Compliance

### 9.1 Regulatory Compliance Frameworks

- **Australia**: Biosecurity Act 2015 and Biosecurity Regulation 2016 provide the legal framework for compliance and enforcement, including powers for biosecurity officers to secure vessels, inspect and take samples, and enforce compliance through civil penalties, infringement notices, enforceable undertakings, and injunctions. [Legislation.gov.au](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2015A00061/2024-10-14/2024-10-14/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html)

- **California BCAP**: The Biosecurity Compliance Audit Program requires poultry farms in buffer zones and previously infected farms to pass biosecurity audits before moving poultry. Audits review biosecurity plans, perimeter security, lines of separation, rodent and wildlife control, documentation, and training compliance. Virtual audits are required every 6 months after initial in-person audit. [CDFA](https://www.cdfa.ca.gov/AHFSS/Animal_Health/docs/BCAP_Factsheet.pdf)

### 9.2 Audit Requirements

Biosecurity audits assess:
- Written biosecurity plan, policies, and procedures (submitted at least 2 days prior)
- Visual verification that on-site matches written plan (perimeter security, line of separation, rodent & wildlife control)
- Documentation and training compliance (employee biosecurity training records, cleaning/disinfection logs, movement tracking)

[CDFA](https://www.cdfa.ca.gov/AHFSS/Animal_Health/docs/BCAP_Factsheet.pdf)

### 9.3 Indemnity and Compliance Linkage

Under USDA APHIS's new indemnity strategy (as of December 31, 2024), poultry producers are required to follow the Biosecurity Compliance Audit Program (BCAP) to receive indemnity payments if they become infected by HPAI. A passing biosecurity audit conducted by a BCAP certified auditor is required before poultry can be moved onto a buffer zone farm or previously infected farm. [CDFA](https://www.cdfa.ca.gov/AHFSS/Animal_Health/docs/BCAP_Factsheet.pdf)

## 10. Bottlenecks

1. **Fragmented governance**: Governance systems remain fragmented across national boundaries, with no unified international framework for DURC oversight. The U.S. DURC-PEPP Policy implementation was superseded by Executive Order 14,292, leaving a policy vacuum. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1924238/full)

2. **Sequence-based screening vulnerability**: BSS tools rely on sequence identity to known SOCs, which can be evaded by AI-assisted protein design producing sequence-divergent synthetic homologs. Two of four tested tools could detect fragments as short as 50 nucleotides, but effectiveness is expected to decline as AIPD capabilities improve. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1858951/full) | [bioRxiv, 2026](https://biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.04.709671v1.full-text)

3. **Decentralized DNA synthesis**: Benchtop DNA synthesizers are becoming accessible to smaller laboratories and non-institutional actors, fundamentally reshaping the threat landscape and making list-based screening approaches insufficient. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1924238/full)

4. **Cyberbiosecurity gap**: The convergence of cybersecurity and biosecurity is not yet consistently integrated into DURC oversight frameworks, despite increasing reliance on networked instruments and automated systems. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1924238/full)

5. **Insider threat mitigation**: Insider threats remain a critical but underaddressed challenge in biosecurity governance, requiring dedicated mitigation strategies within institutional frameworks. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1924238/full)

6. **One-size-fits-all standards**: Generalised standards are a key weakness of current biosecurity; implementation must be tailored to local contexts, environments, and systems. [PMC, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10451226)

7. **Single-disease focus**: Most biosecurity initiatives target single diseases and traditional livestock species, failing to address the wide range of hazards that can be prevented by applying biosecurity. [PMC, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10451226)

8. **Capability assessment gap**: There is limited evidence of the factors or elements that support the progressive and sustainable scaling up of biosecurity along value chains from local to global levels. [PMC, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10451226)

9. **Cost recovery sustainability**: Biosecurity funding relies heavily on cost recovery mechanisms that may not keep pace with evolving threats, with charges increasing only by legislated indexation (3.8% in 2026-27). [DAFF, 2026-27](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/2026-27-fees-charges.pdf)

10. **Autonomous lab governance gap**: Rapid scaling of autonomous labs (e.g., Ginkgo's Nebula) outpaces governance frameworks for overseeing AI-driven biological design and synthesis at scale. [SEC Filing, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1830214/000162828026032093/ex991earningspr.htm)

## 11. Failure Modes

1. **AI-enabled screening evasion**: AIPD tools can produce synthetic homologs of proteins of concern with minimal sequence identity, potentially evading sequence-based BSS and obtaining proteins with hazardous functions (toxins, viral proteins). [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1858951/full)

2. **Fragment-based evasion**: Fragmentation of synthetic homologs into smaller segments can evade screening; only 2 of 4 tested BSS tools robustly detected fragments as short as 50 nucleotides. [bioRxiv, 2026](https://biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.04.709671v1.full-text)

3. **Closed-loop automation risk**: Level 5 IAB systems (full AI-Bio automation integration) enable rapid, automated, and potentially remote execution of complex bioengineering tasks with minimal human oversight, maximizing both potential for benefit and potential for misuse. [Frontiers in Microbiology, 2025](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2025.1734561/full)

4. **Cyberbiosecurity breach**: Cyber-attacks can manipulate experimental conditions, corrupt safety systems, or exfiltrate sensitive DURC data from networked laboratory instruments. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1924238/full)

5. **Insider threat exploitation**: Insiders with legitimate access to high-containment facilities and sensitive biological materials can bypass external security measures. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1924238/full)

6. **Policy vacuum**: The supersession of the U.S. DURC-PEPP Policy by Executive Order 14,292 creates a regulatory gap while a revised or replacement federal policy remains under development. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1924238/full)

7. **Dual-use convergence**: The same platforms that enable vaccine development, enzyme engineering, or sustainable materials research can be redirected toward harmful ends, making dual-use potential inherent in mainstream life science research. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1819026/pdf)

8. **Audit failure cascades**: Failure to pass biosecurity audits can result in inability to collect indemnity payments, move poultry onto farms, or restock previously infected facilities, creating economic incentives for non-compliance. [CDFA](https://www.cdfa.ca.gov/AHFSS/Animal_Health/docs/BCAP_Factsheet.pdf)

## 12. Cost Tradeoffs

1. **Screening stringency vs. throughput**: More stringent screening (e.g., fragment-level detection, function-based prediction) increases costs and may slow legitimate research, while less stringent screening increases biosecurity risk. [bioRxiv, 2026](https://biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.04.709671v1.full-text)

2. **Centralized vs. decentralized synthesis**: Centralized DNA synthesis enables more effective screening but limits access and increases costs; decentralized benchtop synthesis lowers costs but fundamentally reshapes the threat landscape. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1924238/full)

3. **Automation vs. human oversight**: Full automation (Level 5 IAB) maximizes throughput and efficiency but minimizes human oversight, creating a tradeoff between productivity and biosecurity control. [Frontiers in Microbiology, 2025](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2025.1734561/full)

4. **Cost recovery vs. accessibility**: Biosecurity cost recovery charges (increasing 3.8% annually) may create barriers to compliance for smaller operators while funding the biosecurity system. [DAFF, 2026-27](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/2026-27-fees-charges.pdf)

5. **Biosecurity investment vs. divestiture**: Ginkgo Bioworks divested its biosecurity business to focus on autonomous labs, reflecting a market tradeoff between biosecurity services and autonomous research infrastructure. [SEC Filing, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1830214/000162828026032093/ex991earningspr.htm)

6. **Tailored vs. standardized approaches**: Tailored biosecurity approaches specific to local contexts are more effective but more costly to develop and implement than standardized one-size-fits-all solutions. [PMC, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10451226)

7. **Watermarking vs. open science**: Protein watermarking enables identification of AI-designed proteins but may conflict with open science norms and add complexity to the research workflow. [Ars Technica, 2026](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/09/google-figures-out-how-to-watermark-ai-designed-proteins)

## 15. NP-Hard Problems

1. **Sequence-function prediction**: Predicting a protein's function directly from its sequence (rather than via sequence identity correlates) is computationally intractable for arbitrary sequences, requiring advances in protein structure prediction and function annotation. [bioRxiv, 2026](https://biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.04.709671v1.full-text)

2. **Optimal screening threshold determination**: Balancing false positives (flagging benign sequences) against false negatives (missing hazardous sequences) across an exponentially large sequence space is an optimization problem with no known polynomial-time solution. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1858951/full)

3. **Fitness landscape exploration**: The sheer number of possible mutations, even within a single protein, makes exhaustive experimental or computational testing infeasible. Active learning offers a solution but optimal experiment selection remains computationally hard. [Frontiers in Microbiology, 2025](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2025.1734561/full)

4. **Multi-objective biosecurity governance**: Optimizing across competing objectives—research productivity, biosecurity safety, cost efficiency, and international collaboration—across multiple jurisdictions with different regulatory frameworks is a combinatorial optimization problem. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1924238/full)

5. **Cyberbiosecurity threat modeling**: Modeling the attack surface of networked laboratory instruments and automated systems against adversarial actors with evolving capabilities is an open problem in security analysis. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1924238/full)

## 17. SOTA Approaches

### 17.1 AI-Biology Integration Levels

| Level | Description | Risk |
|-------|-------------|------|
| Level 1: Zero-shot prediction | Basic pLM predictions (e.g., sequence likelihood as fitness proxy) | Low–Moderate |
| Level 2: Advanced prediction & analysis | Accurate ML/pLM prediction of complex molecular properties | Moderate |
| Level 3: Targeted sequence generation | Generative AI/pLMs designing novel sequences for specific functions | High |
| Level 4: Integrated design & active learning | Combining generative models with active learning/Bayesian optimization | Very High |
| Level 5: Full AI-Bio automation | Closed-loop DBTL systems with minimal human oversight | Extremely High |

[Frontiers in Microbiology, 2025](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2025.1734561/full)

### 17.2 Screening Tool Evolution

- **Current**: Sequence identity-based screening (BSS tools)
- **Patched**: AI-resilient BSS tools capable of detecting fragments as short as 50 nucleotides
- **Future**: Function-based screening approaches that predict sequence function directly rather than via sequence identity correlates

[bioRxiv, 2026](https://biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.04.709671v1.full-text)

### 17.3 Protein Watermarking

Google DeepMind's SynthID-based protein watermarking represents a novel approach to tracking AI-designed proteins. The watermark is distributed throughout the protein sequence, cannot be removed without knowing the encoding, and does not compromise protein function. [Ars Technica, 2026](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/09/google-figures-out-how-to-watermark-ai-designed-proteins)

### 17.4 Capability-Oriented Framework

A proposed capability-oriented framework for evaluating integrated AI–biology systems maps capability levels to biosecurity risk, enabling more nuanced governance than binary allowed/prohibited approaches. [Frontiers in Microbiology, 2025](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2025.1734561/full)

## 18. Scalability Limits

1. **Geographic disparity**: Considerable disparities in the geographic distribution of evidence related to biosecurity initiatives, with limited evidence from developing countries. [PMC, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10451226)

2. **Definition inconsistency**: Differences in definitions for biosecurity across sectors and disciplines hinder harmonious understanding and coordinated scaling. [PMC, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10451226)

3. **Value chain complexity**: Biosecurity initiatives often fail to recognise the full range of hazards across complex value chains, focusing instead on single diseases and traditional livestock species. [PMC, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10451226)

4. **Stakeholder fragmentation**: Including all relevant stakeholders—public, private, formal, and informal actors—in biosecurity design is challenging but necessary for sustainable scaling. [PMC, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10451226)

5. **Economic sustainability**: Biosecurity funding models relying on cost recovery may not be sustainable long-term, particularly for developing countries with limited resources. [DAFF, 2023-24](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/biosecurity-funding-expenditure-report-2023-24.pdf)

6. **Technology pace**: The pace of AI and automation advancement (e.g., autonomous labs doubling in size annually) outpaces the development of corresponding governance frameworks. [SEC Filing, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1830214/000162828026032093/ex991earningspr.htm)

7. **International coordination**: No unified international framework exists for DURC oversight, with governance systems fragmented across national boundaries. [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2026](https://frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1924238/full)
